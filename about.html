Our story

Stanford Founders did not start as an organization. It started as a group chat, and it grew because the need was real.

01 —

A group chat

Stanford Founders began as a casual WhatsApp group: ten friends, spread across different graduate schools at Stanford, sharing entrepreneurial events, resources, and opportunities with one another.

There was no charter and no calendar. Someone would hear about a pitch competition in one school, a speaker in another, a grant nobody in their own department had mentioned, and pass it along.

The chat grew from ten friends to more than a thousand members. From our founding story
02 —

Bridging the schools

We noticed the difficulty Stanford graduate students faced in finding cofounders and accessing entrepreneurial resources from other schools. An engineer rarely met the business student two buildings over; a scientist with a promising result had no obvious way to find the operator who could help build a company around it.

The group aimed to bridge that gap. It grew from ten people to 1,150+ members, highlighting the need for the network: the demand was there long before the organization was.

03 —

An official home

We turned the group into an official student organization, offering programs and events for cross-disciplinary Stanford graduate students at the early stage of their startup development.

Today that means programming, co-founder dinners, sector mixers, and introductions across the seven Stanford graduate schools, and a Demo Day each spring that puts our founders in front of investors. Our community of 1,150+ graduate student founders thrives by collaborating; Stanford Founders connects them with resources, investors, and like-minded co-founders across every school.

Where we fit

Early, and across every school.

Stanford has plenty of entrepreneurship infrastructure, including StartX, GSB E-Club, BASES, ASES, STVP, and more. Stanford Founders tries to help early, when graduate students are transitioning from research to startup and seeking a new community.

When
Early. The transition from research to startup, before a team or a company exists.
Who
Graduate students from all seven Stanford schools, not one department.
